The case for paying readers first

Everyone knows early buyers carry the risk. What if the people who believed first earned from everyone who followed?

白
Baizhi Heizi 6 min read · Aug 27
This article is a value-net reward: 40% of new income is split among earlier readers, 50% to the author, 10% to the platform.

The problem with publishing markets

Most Web3 writing platforms hand all the upside to the author while the first readers take the risk. When an article lands, the author collects every purchase after it. The earliest reader — the one who paid before any social proof existed — gets nothing more than the article itself.

Quill asks a different question. Instead of a one-way sale, what if each article is a small economy where readers who went first are repaid by the readers who followed?

Every new payment is split three ways — 50% to the author, 40% pro-rata to earlier readers, 10% to the platform.

How the split works

On every purchase, the early-reader pool is divided proportionally by what each earlier reader paid. If you were the only reader before it got popular, you are the pool. If you tipped, your tip counts too — a tipper is a buyer for future rewards.

Why this matters more than it sounds

Paying first used to be an act of faith. Under a value net, it is an act of ownership. The mechanism aligns the reader's incentive with the author's success, and it compounds: a bigger pool attracts more readers, which makes the early position worth more, which draws more writers.

It is a small reframing with a large consequence. Risk, for the first time, is shared — and rewarded.
